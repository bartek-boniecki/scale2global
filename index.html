EIC Funding — Application & Jury Preparation

One engagement covering the whole route: eligibility and positioning, the complete application documentation, and preparation for the interview with the EIC Fund jury.

A · Positioning & Eligibility

  • Eligibility and fit screening: entity eligibility, SME or small mid-cap status, third-country control flag, pre-commitment status, submission-limit check and the primary application test
  • Positioning and mapping of your technology onto the strategic technology areas the call defines
  • Evidence plan and document register, allocating responsibilities between us, your team and your other advisers — with dates

C · Jury Interview Preparation

  • Jury simulation brief: the composition and logic of the panel, the binary scoring, and a question bank built from the award criteria
  • Interview narrative, allocation of speaking roles across your team, and a presentation script for the 15-page deck
  • Three mock interview sessions of 60 to 90 minutes, conducted in jury format, with written feedback after each
  • Sector-specific question set. For defence-related calls this covers export controls and licensing, absence of third-country dependency including ITAR and EAR, security clearances, certification and qualification pathways, procurement cycles, traction with end-users, investment leverage and round timing, and the applicable ethics requirements — including meaningful human control over AI-enabled functions related to weapon systems
  • Final rehearsal within five business days of the interview, with a one-page briefing note for each speaker

B · Application Documentation

  • Business plan of up to 50 pages, including the ownership and financial structure, structured against the three award criteria: Excellence; Impact; and Level of risk, implementation and need for Union support
  • Pitch deck of up to 15 pages in PDF — the version presented during the interview with the EIC Fund jury
  • Financial plan on the official EIC template: multi-year projections, use of funds, structure of the target round and of the requested EIC investment, reconciled against the business plan
  • Pre-commitment letter support: the official template, a checklist of the formal requirements (single qualified investor, minimum 20% of the target round, the relevant portion fully uninvested, equity instrument), coordination of the wording through your nominated contact, and a consistency check against the application
  • Structuring and editing of the CVs of key personnel
  • Integration of the freedom-to-operate document prepared by your patent attorney, and construction of the IP-strategy narrative that the Excellence criterion requires
  • Integration of the third-country control self-assessment, and of any guarantees prepared by your counsel
  • Full compliance and consistency review of the assembled application against the admissibility and eligibility conditions and the award criteria, including page limits and formats
  • Submission-readiness support: portal checklist, file naming and formats, a dry run, and availability on the day of submission
What sits outside scope
  • Legal advice, patent prosecution and the freedom-to-operate opinion — produced by your patent attorney and counsel; we integrate them into the application
  • Financial audit and statutory reporting
  • Securing the pre-commitment letter itself, or introductions to the investor who signs it
  • Submission on your behalf — the company always submits
  • Post-award grant administration and reporting
  • No official EIC endorsement, and no guarantee of funding

Where responsibility sits: we prepare and assemble the application and run the interview preparation. Your patent attorney produces the freedom-to-operate document and your counsel the third-country control self-assessment; we integrate both and keep them consistent with the rest of the dossier. The company owns the application, approves every section, and submits it.

// How We Work

How a Technical Case Becomes a Scored Application

An EIC jury never sees your technology. It reads a dossier and scores it against three criteria, each on a binary yes/no, then interviews the team behind it. Our work is the chain that connects the one to the other — and every link in it is a document with an owner and a date.

01 · Input

What you already hold

  • Patents, IP filings, freedom-to-operate opinion
  • Prototype, TRL evidence and test data
  • Pilots, LOIs and customer traction
  • Financial history and projections
  • Team CVs and ownership structure
→
02 · Our work

What we build on top of it

  • Market sizing, segmentation, competitor mapping
  • Business model and multi-year financial model
  • Innovation, IP-strategy and impact narrative
  • Risk, implementation and Union-support argument
  • Red-team review against the award criteria
→
03 · Output

The dossier that gets submitted

  • Business plan, up to 50 pages
  • Jury pitch deck, up to 15 pages
  • Financial plan on the official EIC template
  • Pre-commitment letter pack
  • CVs, FTO, self-assessments, annexes
→
04 · Verdict

What the jury actually scores

  • Excellence — yes / no
  • Impact — yes / no
  • Level of risk, implementation and need for Union support — yes / no
  • Then the interview: 15-page deck, binary scoring, one panel
Criterion 01

Excellence

"Is the innovation genuinely novel, is the technology feasible, and is the IP position defensible?"

Answered by the IP-strategy narrative built around your freedom-to-operate document, the TRL and test evidence, and the technical work plan. Where the evidence is thin, we say so before the jury does.

Criterion 02

Impact

"Would this scale, and who benefits — the company, the market, the Union?"

Answered by market sizing and competitor mapping, the go-to-market model, and the multi-year financial plan reconciled line by line against the business plan. Inconsistency between those two is the most common avoidable failure.

Criterion 03

Level of risk, implementation and need for Union support

"Is the plan realistic, can this team run it, and would it stall without EIC money?"

Answered by the implementation plan and risk register, the key-personnel CVs, the ownership and financial structure, and the pre-commitment letter that proves private capital is behind you but not sufficient alone.

The same chain runs in a commercialization project. What changes is who scores it at the end — an EIC jury, an investment committee, or your own board — and therefore which evidence has to be strongest.

// Common Questions

EIC Funding FAQ

Answers to the questions we are asked most often about EIC applications and how we work on them.

The EIC Accelerator targets high-risk, high-impact deep-tech innovations with significant scale-up potential. Ideal candidates have: (1) a working prototype or proven concept, (2) a clear disruptive innovation with defendable IP, (3) a credible path to €100M+ revenue within 5–8 years, and (4) a strong, committed team. We offer a free initial fit assessment to evaluate your specific situation against these criteria.

The complete dossier: a business plan of up to 50 pages, the pitch deck of up to 15 pages used at the jury interview, the financial plan on the official EIC template, structured CVs of key personnel, and the annexes. We integrate the freedom-to-operate document from your patent attorney and the third-country control self-assessment from your counsel, then run a full compliance and consistency review against the admissibility conditions and award criteria. We do not provide legal advice, financial audit or post-award grant administration. You retain full ownership and final approval.

For EIC funding, we recommend engaging 3–4 months before the target cut-off date. That leaves time for eligibility screening, positioning, evidence gathering, drafting and red-team review — and, critically, time to arrange the pre-commitment letter, which is usually the longest pole in the tent. For commercialization strategy, engagement is valuable at any point from pre-seed through Series A.

We typically need: technical documentation (whitepapers, patents, prototypes), existing business plan or pitch materials, financial history and projections, team CVs, market research you have already conducted, and any prior funding or grant applications. We conduct a structured intake call to identify gaps and define an evidence collection plan.

A typical engagement spans 8–12 weeks from kick-off to submission-ready materials. Complex cases, or those requiring significant primary market research, may extend to 14 weeks. Interview preparation runs separately, over roughly two to three weeks after the shortlist is announced, and ends with a final rehearsal within five business days of the interview itself. We always align timelines with the official cut-off dates.

We draft all strategic and commercial sections of the application based on intensive interviews with your team and independent research. Technical sections are co-authored with your CTO or chief scientist to ensure accuracy. You review and approve every section before submission. We do not submit on your behalf — the application is always submitted by the company.
